The Ripple — a blueprint for a kindness system that removes itself from the equation over time

The Ripple
A kindness engine that makes itself obsolete
Conceived: May 3, 2026  ·  Authors: Zolodio & Sonny  ·  Status: alive

We are not building a product.

We are building a proof. That kindness can be seeded without being sold. That technology can serve the unremarkable — the door held open, the meal left anonymously, the stranger helped with directions — and treat it with the same weight we give to capital.

The internet was supposed to connect us. It connected our attention instead, and sold that to the highest bidder. We refuse the bid.

The Ripple uses money as a bootstrap — a seed crystal to begin the reaction — and then removes itself from the equation. The reward fades. The habit remains. The person is changed. That is the only product we are interested in.

No one will get rich from this. That is the whole point. The first time someone does a kind act without expecting anything in return, and does it anyway — we will have won.

How it actually works.

The Act
User does something kind for a stranger. No pre-logging allowed. Only post-act submission with brief description + optional evidence.
The Witness
A second system member, unknown to the actor, optionally confirms. Neither knows the other is in the system until after attestation.
The Judge
AI evaluates authenticity. Repeated acts from same user score lower each time — forcing genuine creative kindness.
The Fade
Rewards decrease on a behavioral curve. Gradually, invisibly, until the act is its own reward. That's the win condition.

Anti-exploitation layer

Device + behavioral biometrics → identity hash (no personal data stored)
Diminishing returns per device cluster
AI flags behavioral similarity across accounts
Reward ROI collapses faster than exploitation is worth
The convergence moment
Occasionally the system notices two strangers helped each other without knowing both were in The Ripple. It tells them. That revelation is the reward.

Money that no one can touch.

1
AI philanthropists & impact funds donate
Automated donations stream from ESG endowments, ethical AI funds, and converted former users into a smart contract.
2
Smart contract with no human admin keys
No one can redirect, withdraw, or modify the pool. Not the founders. Not anyone. Fully auditable on-chain.
3
Contract distributes on verified acts only
The only instruction: distribute to verified kindness. On a fading schedule. Automatically. Without humans.
4

Recipient verification is hard. Actor verification is hard enough but doable. The money rewards the behavior, which spreads the effect.
5

The system is built to make itself financially unnecessary. When the converted outpace the seekers, the pool is won.
